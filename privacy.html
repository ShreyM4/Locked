Privacy Policy for Browser Lock

Last updated: August 27, 2026

🔒 100% Offline • Zero Data Collection • Open Source

1. Overview

Browser Lock ("we", "our", or "the extension") is a privacy-first, open-source security extension designed to password-protect your Google Chrome profile session.

Your privacy is our core principle. Browser Lock is engineered with a strict Zero-Knowledge & Zero-Telemetry Architecture. We do not operate any external tracking servers, analytics endpoints, or remote databases.

2. What Data We Collect

We collect, store, transmit, and sell absolutely NO personal data or browsing information.

Specifically:

  • No Browsing Activity: We cannot see, track, or access your browsing history, visited URLs, web page contents, downloads, or bookmarks.
  • No Network Communication: The extension makes zero external network requests. Everything executes completely offline within your browser's local sandbox.
  • No Analytics or Telemetry: No tracking cookies, diagnostic beacons, or behavioral identifiers are used.

3. Local Data Storage & Security

All authentication credentials and security settings remain exclusively on your physical device inside Chrome's isolated local extension storage (chrome.storage.local):

  • PIN Storage: Your PIN is never saved in plaintext. It is cryptographically salted (16 bytes random salt) and hashed using PBKDF2-SHA256 with 600,000 iterations via the native Web Crypto API.
  • 2FA Recovery Secret: The TOTP secret key is generated locally on your machine using cryptographically secure random values and is verified entirely offline according to RFC 6238 standards.

4. Chrome Permissions Transparency

Browser Lock requests only the minimum permissions necessary for its lock screen and recovery features:

  • storage: To persist your encrypted PIN hash, TOTP recovery key, and preferences locally.
  • alarms: To schedule brute-force lockout cooldowns and auto-lock inactivity timers.
  • identity & identity.email: Used strictly in a local context to retrieve your current Chrome profile's Google account email, allowing Google Authenticator to display a clear label (e.g. BrowserLock (user@gmail.com)). This email is never transmitted or shared.
  • idle (Optional): Used only if you choose to enable the inactivity auto-lock feature in settings to detect when your computer is idle.

5. Open Source Transparency

Browser Lock is free, open-source software under the MIT License. The complete source code is publicly auditable on GitHub, allowing anyone to verify our offline operation and cryptographic integrity.

6. Contact & Inquiries

If you have any questions, feedback, or security inquiries regarding this Privacy Policy or the extension, please open an issue on our GitHub repository.
