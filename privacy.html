PRIVACY NOTICE

Last updated: August 27, 2026

This privacy notice for Browser Lock ("we", "us", or "our") describes how and why we might collect, store, use, and/or process your information when you use our Chrome Extension.

1. WHAT INFORMATION DO WE COLLECT?

In Short: We do not collect, transmit, sell, or share any personal information or browsing data.

Browser Lock operates 100% locally and offline in your browser:

  • No Browsing History: We do not collect or monitor your visited websites, URLs, search queries, or page content.
  • No Personal Identifiers: We do not collect names, addresses, phone numbers, or device identifiers.
  • No Remote Communication: The extension makes zero external network connections and contains no tracking code, analytics, or telemetry of any kind.
  • No Accounts: The extension does not require you to sign up, log in, or create any account with us.

2. HOW IS YOUR DATA STORED?

All data created by the extension is stored strictly on your local machine, inside Chrome's local storage sandbox (chrome.storage.local). Nothing is ever synced to a server we control.

  • PIN Data: Your PIN is never stored in plaintext. It is cryptographically salted (unique 16-byte random salt) and hashed using PBKDF2-SHA256 with 600,000 iterations, directly on your device.
  • 2FA Recovery Secret: The TOTP secret is generated randomly on your device and validated locally according to the standard offline RFC 6238 protocol, compatible with apps like Google Authenticator, Authy, Aegis, and 1Password.
  • Manual Backups: If you choose to export a binary recovery key file or copy your Base32 secret, that file is saved directly to a location you choose on your own disk. We have no access to it.
  • Preferences: Your shortcut, auto-lock timeout, and other settings are also stored locally.

3. WHAT PERMISSIONS DO WE USE AND WHY?

The extension requires the following browser permissions solely to provide its lock screen functionality:

  • storage: Used to save your encrypted PIN hash, recovery secret, and preferences on your device.
  • alarms: Used to handle brute-force lockout delay timers and inactivity auto-lock timers.
  • identity & identity.email: Used strictly to read your local Chrome profile's email address so that your authenticator app can display an identifiable label (e.g. "Browser Lock — you@example.com"). This information is used locally only and is never transmitted anywhere.
  • idle (optional): Used only if you choose to enable the inactivity auto-lock feature in Settings.

4. THIRD-PARTY DISCLOSURE

We do not sell, trade, rent, or transfer your information to outside parties, because no such information ever reaches us in the first place. No data leaves your local computer sandbox.

5. DATA RETENTION & DELETION

Data is retained locally for as long as the extension is installed. Uninstalling Browser Lock from chrome://extensions permanently deletes its local storage, including your PIN hash, salt, TOTP secret, and settings. Any manually exported backup files remain on your disk until you delete them yourself.

6. OPEN SOURCE & VERIFICATION

Browser Lock is free, open-source software released under the MIT License. The full source code is publicly available on GitHub at github.com/ShreyM4/Locked for independent security and privacy audits, so you can verify every claim in this notice for yourself.

7. CHILDREN'S PRIVACY

Browser Lock does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children, as it collects no information from any user under any circumstance.

8. CHANGES TO THIS NOTICE

We may update this notice from time to time. The updated version will be indicated by a revised "Last updated" date at the top of this page.

9. CONTACT US

If you have questions or comments about this notice, you may contact us by opening an issue on our GitHub repository.
